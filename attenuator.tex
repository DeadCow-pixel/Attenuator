\begin{abstract}
    We study the security of continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD) under receiver asymmetry within an untrusted-detector model. We analyze additive-noise and attenuator channel representations of the same target POVM for each detection scheme. For homodyne and double-homodyne detection, we derive the corresponding channel parameters and compare the resulting Holevo information and asymptotic secret fraction.
  
  For the parameter sets considered, receiver asymmetry reduces the asymptotic secret fraction for both effective channels. The channel giving the lower asymptotic secret fraction depends on the receiver parameters and the transmittance of the communication channel. For homodyne detection, the attenuator channel gives a lower asymptotic secret fraction at high communication-channel transmittance. Its secret fraction remains positive over a longer distance.

  In the double-homodyne case, the channel parameters, the Holevo information, and asymptotic secret fraction depend on the squeezing parameter $r$ that labels the reference Gaussian POVM. %, whereas the Alice-Bob mutual information calculated from the calibrated receiver statistics does not.
  For the parameter sets considered, the Holevo information as a function of $r$ is concave for the additive-noise channel and convex for the attenuator channel. The choice of $r$ and the effective channel therefore affect the resulting asymptotic secret fraction.
\end{abstract}

Homodyne and double-homodyne detection are standard coherent detection schemes in quantum optics and continuous-variable quantum information~\cite{Braunstein:rmp:2005,Weed:rmp:2012}. Here double-homodyne detection denotes the two-branch receiver that implements the conventional heterodyne measurement. In the strong local oscillator limit, an ideal homodyne receiver implements a projective measurement of one field quadrature. An ideal balanced double-homodyne receiver jointly (but necessarily with noise) estimates two conjugate quadratures through a coherent-state positive operator-valued measure (POVM), whose effects are proportional to coherent-state projectors~\cite{Weed:rmp:2012,HolevoGaussianObservable2021}. Both schemes implement Gaussian POVMs and give Gaussian outcome distributions for Gaussian input states. They are used in quantum state tomography~\cite{d2003quantum,Lvovsky:rmp:2009}, quantum state preparation~\cite{Zhang_2019}, and continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD)~\cite{Grosshans:prl:2002,Pirandola:20,Zhang:apr:2024}.

Arthurs and Kelly derived an uncertainty relation for the output variances of unbiased simultaneous measurements of conjugate observables~\cite{ArthursKelly1965}. For two canonical quadratures, an explicit construction introduces an auxiliary mode prepared in a fixed state and measures two commuting quadrature combinations~\cite{Holevo:problemy:1972}. For a zero-mean Gaussian state with equal variances in the two quadratures and no quadrature correlations, Holevo showed that the coherent-state POVM, implemented using an auxiliary mode in the vacuum state, minimizes the sum of the mean-square errors in the joint measurement of the two quadratures~\cite{Holevo1973Statistical}. Yuen and Chan later analyzed the mean-square noise spectra of optical homodyne and heterodyne detection, distinguishing signal quantum fluctuations from local-oscillator excess noise~\cite{Yuen:ol:1983}. Holevo's classification of Gaussian POVMs includes noiseless and noisy homodyne measurements and heterodyne measurements with vacuum or thermal noise~\cite{HolevoGaussianObservable2021}.

Practical receivers may have nonunit and unequal detector quantum efficiencies, as well as beam-splitter imbalance. Within the Gaussian approximation to the photocount statistics in the strong local oscillator limit, these imperfections yield noisy Gaussian POVMs~\cite{lahti2010realistic,Barchielli_2022,naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne}. These receiver imperfections enter CV-QKD parameter estimation and the calculation of the asymptotic secret fraction~\cite{Pereira2021,PRXQuantum.3.010341,Mi2024BiasedDetection}. We use \emph{receiver asymmetry} for the combined effect of beam-splitter imbalance and unequal detector quantum efficiencies. For each detection scheme, the receiver parameters determine the noisy Gaussian POVM to be reproduced, which we call the target POVM. A channel representation of the target POVM consists of an effective one-mode Gaussian channel $\mathcal{C}$ acting after the communication channel, a reference Gaussian POVM applied to its output, and an invertible deterministic rescaling of the measurement outcomes.

Our previous work derived the target POVMs for receiver asymmetry in homodyne and double-homodyne detection and constructed additive-noise channel representations of these POVMs~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne}. For double-homodyne detection, we used a family of rank-one reference Gaussian POVMs whose effects are proportional to projectors onto displaced squeezed states. The squeezing parameter $r$ labels this family, with $r=0$ corresponding to the coherent-state POVM of conventional double-homodyne detection. Complete positivity of the additive-noise channel restricts $r$ to an interval determined by the receiver parameters. We denote the sender and receiver by Alice and Bob, respectively. In that analysis, an eavesdropper (Eve) was assumed to purify their joint state after the effective receiver channel, and the resulting Holevo information depends on $r$. The same target POVM can therefore admit several channel representations. Although these representations give the same outcome statistics, they can produce different Alice--Bob states after the effective receiver channel and hence different Holevo information.

In this paper, we construct attenuator channel representations of the target homodyne and double-homodyne POVMs and compare them with the corresponding additive-noise channel representations. For each detection scheme, we determine the channel parameters by requiring the dual maps, together with an invertible deterministic rescaling of the outcomes, to map the corresponding reference POVMs to the target POVM. By construction, the two representations reproduce the same receiver statistics after rescaling for every input state, and the invertibility of the rescaling preserves the Alice--Bob mutual information. We then compare the resulting Holevo information and asymptotic secret fraction for the Gaussian-modulated coherent-state (GMCS) protocol with reverse reconciliation under collective Gaussian attacks~\cite{Grosshans:prl:2002,Navascues2006GaussianAttacks,GarciaPatron2006GaussianAttacks}.

The security interpretation of a channel representation depends on how receiver noise is treated. In a trusted-detector model, Eve has no access to the environmental output of the effective receiver channel~\cite{Lodewyck:pra:2007,Usenko:entropy:2016,Laudenbach:aqt:2019,Namiki2018DetectionChannel}. For a fixed joint state of Alice, Bob, and Eve before that channel, two representations of the same target POVM give the same joint state of Eve and Bob's classical outcome and therefore the same Holevo information. In the untrusted-detector model adopted here, Eve is instead assumed to purify the Alice--Bob state after the effective receiver channel. Since the additive-noise and attenuator channels generally produce different Alice--Bob states, they can yield different Holevo information and asymptotic secret fractions despite reproducing the same receiver statistics. Selecting the effective receiver channel therefore introduces an additional physical assumption in the security analysis.

\section{Target Gaussian POVMs}\label{sec:POVMs}
We now specify the target homodyne and double-homodyne POVMs produced by receiver asymmetry, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:scheme}. Receiver asymmetry arises from beam-splitter imbalance and unequal detector quantum efficiencies. Ref.~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne} gives the detailed derivations used in this section.

For the homodyne receiver, $\hat a$ is the annihilation operator of the signal mode, $\alpha$ and $\alpha_L$ are the coherent amplitudes of the signal and local oscillator (LO), and $C$ and $S$ are real beam-splitter amplitude coefficients satisfying $C^2+S^2=1$. The photodetectors have quantum efficiencies $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$, and $\mu=m_1-m_2$ is their photon-count difference.

The $Q$ symbol of the target homodyne POVM (see Fig.~\ref{subfig:homodyne}) is
\begin{align}
  &\label{eq:Pgood-w-def}
    {\prob}_G^{(\ind{H})}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_x}}
    \exp \left[-\frac{(x-\avr{\hat{x}_\phi})^2}{2\sigma_x}\right],\\
  &\label{eq:avr-x-phi}
  \avr{\hat{x}_\phi}\equiv \bra{\alpha}\hat{x}_\phi\ket{\alpha}=2\Re(\alpha e^{-i\phi}),
  \quad \phi=\arg\alpha_L,\\
    &\label{eq:quad-op}
    \hat{x}_\phi=\hat{a}e^{-i\phi}+\hat{a}^\dag e^{i\phi},
\end{align}
where $\hat{x}_\phi$ is the phase-rotated quadrature operator. The rescaled quadrature variable is
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:x-def}
  x\equiv\frac{\mu}{(\eta_1+\eta_2)CS\left|\alpha_L\right|}-\frac{\eta_1S^2-\eta_2C^2}{\left(\eta_1+\eta_2\right)CS}\left|\alpha_L\right|,
\end{align}
and its variance is
\begin{align}
  &
        \label{eq:sigma-x}
    \sigma_x\equiv
    \frac{\eta_1S^2+\eta_2C^2}{
    \left[(\eta_1+\eta_2)CS\right]^2
    }.
\end{align}

Taking the inverse Fourier transform of the $Q$ symbol~\eqref{eq:Pgood-w-def} gives the antinormally ordered characteristic function and determines the target covariance matrix $\Sigma_m^{(\ind{H})}$~\cite{Serafini:bk:2023}. We introduce the reference covariance matrix $\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{H})}$ by setting $\sigma_N=0$. With $r$ as an auxiliary squeezing parameter, the two covariance matrices are %(see also Ref.~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne} for derivation)
\begin{align}
    &\label{eq:Sigma-H-m}
    \Sigma_{m}^{(\ind{H})}= \lim_{r\to+\infty}R(\phi)\,\diag(e^{-2r}+\sigma_N,e^{2r})\,\tcnj{R}(\phi)
    %=\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{H})}+\Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{H})}
    ,\\
    &\label{eq:Sigma-H-id}
    \Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{H})}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}R(\phi)\,\diag(e^{-2r},e^{2r})\,\tcnj{R}(\phi)%\\
    %&\label{eq:Sigma-N-H}
    %\Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{H})}=R(\phi)\,\ind{diag}(\sigma_N,0)\,\tcnj{R}(\phi)
    ,
\end{align}
where $\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{H})}$ is the covariance matrix of the reference homodyne POVM, $R(\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi\\ \sin\phi& \cos\phi\end{pmatrix}$ is the rotation matrix, and $\sigma_N$ is the receiver excess-noise variance,
\begin{align}
  &
  \label{eq:sgm_N}
     \sigma_N=\sigma_x-1.
\end{align}

  \label{fig:scheme}
\end{figure}

For double-homodyne detection, $(C_S,S_S)$ and $(C_L,S_L)$ are the real amplitude coefficients of the signal and LO beam splitters, while $(C_i,S_i)$ are those of homodyne branch $i\in\{1,2\}$. Each pair obeys $C_j^2+S_j^2=1$ for $j\in\{S,L,1,2\}$. The quantities $\eta_k^{(i)}$ and $m_k^{(i)}$ are the quantum efficiency and photon count of detector $k\in\{1,2\}$ in branch $i$, and $\mu_i=m_1^{(i)}-m_2^{(i)}$.

The $Q$ symbol of the target double-homodyne POVM is
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:P_G-x1x2}
  &
    \prob_G^{(\ind{DH})}(x_1,x_2)
    =\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{\sigma_1\sigma_2}}
    \exp\biggl[
    -\frac{[x_1-\Re(\alpha e^{-i\phi})]^2}{\sigma_1}
    -\frac{[x_2-\Im(\alpha e^{-i\phi})]^2}{\sigma_2}
    \biggr],
\end{align}
where $\alpha_L^{(1)}=C_L\alpha_L$ and $\alpha_L^{(2)}=-iS_L\alpha_L$ are the local-oscillator amplitudes in the two homodyne branches, and $x_i$ are the rescaled quadrature variables:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:x-1}
  x_1&=
  \frac{1}{2(\eta_1^{(1)}+\eta_2^{(1)})C_1S_1C_S}
  \left\{
  \frac{\mu_1}{|\alpha_L^{(1)}|}-(\eta_1^{(1)}S_1^2-\eta_2^{(1)}C_1^2) |\alpha_L^{(1)}|
    \right\},
  \\
  \label{eq:x-2}
  x_2&=
  \frac{1}{2(\eta_1^{(2)}+\eta_2^{(2)})C_2S_2S_S}
  \left\{
  \frac{\mu_2}{|\alpha_L^{(2)}|}-(\eta_1^{(2)}S_2^2-\eta_2^{(2)}C_2^2) |\alpha_L^{(2)}|
    \right\}.
\end{align}
The parameters $\sigma_i$ are defined by
\begin{align}
  &
  \label{eq:sigm-i}
    \sigma_1=\frac{\sigma_x^{(1)}}{ 2 C_S^2},
    \quad
    \sigma_2=\frac{\sigma_x^{(2)}}{ 2 S_S^2},
  \\
  &
    \label{eq:sigm-x-i}
    \sigma_x^{(i)}=\frac{\eta_1^{(i)}S_i^2+\eta_2^{(i)}C_i^2}{
    \left[(\eta_1^{(i)}+\eta_2^{(i)})C_i S_i\right]^2
    }.
\end{align}
Thus, for fixed input amplitude $\alpha$, the conditional outcome variances are $\operatorname{Var}(x_i\mid\alpha)=\sigma_i/2$.

Following the same procedure used to derive Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma-H-m}, we obtain the covariance matrix of the target double-homodyne POVM,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Sigma_m-DH}
  &
  \Sigma_m^{(\ind{DH})}=R(\phi)\diag(\delta_1,\delta_2)\tcnj{R}(\phi),
  \\
  \label{eq:sigma-i}
  &
    \delta_i=2\sigma_i-1.
\end{align}

For a complex outcome $z=z_1+iz_2$, we define the effects of the rank-one reference Gaussian POVM by
\begin{align}
  \hat\Pi_{\ind{id}}^{(z)}
  =\frac{1}{\pi}\ket{z,r,\phi}\bra{z,r,\phi},
  \qquad z_1,z_2\in\mathbb{R},
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:squeezed-def}
  \ket{z,r,\phi}=\hat{R}(\phi)\hat{D}(z)\hat{S}(r)\ket{0}=
  \hat{D}(z e^{i\phi})\hat{S}(r e ^{2i\phi})\ket{0},
\end{align}
and $r\in\mathbb{R}$ is the squeezing parameter. The operators are $\hat R(\phi)=e^{i\phi\hat a^\dagger\hat a}$, $\hat D(z)=e^{z\hat a^\dagger-z^*\hat a}$, and $\hat S(\zeta)=e^{[\zeta\hat a^{\dagger2}-\zeta^*\hat a^2]/2}$ for $\zeta\in\mathbb{C}$. At $r=0$, the reference Gaussian POVM reduces to the coherent-state POVM implemented by conventional ideal double-homodyne detection.

For fixed $r$ and $\phi$, the displaced squeezed states in Eq.~\eqref{eq:squeezed-def} have covariance matrix
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:Sigma-id-DH}
  &
  \Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{DH})}=
    R(\phi)
    \begin{pmatrix}
      e^{2r}&0\\0&e^{-2r}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \tcnj{R}(\phi).
\end{align}

Complete positivity of the additive-noise channel considered below requires $\Sigma_m^{(\ind{DH})}-\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{DH})}\succeq0$. Equations~\eqref{eq:Sigma_m-DH} and~\eqref{eq:Sigma-id-DH} therefore restrict $r$ to the interval fixed by the receiver parameters~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne},
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:r1-r2}
  &
    r\in \left[r_2,r_1\right],\;
    r_1=\ln\delta_1^{1/2},
    \;
    r_2=\ln\delta_2^{-1/2}.
\end{align}

\section{Gaussian channels modeling imperfect measurements}
\label{sec:channels}

Throughout this section, we consider Gaussian channels with zero displacement and zero-mean noise and use units in which the vacuum covariance matrix is $\mathbbm{1}$. For measurement type $\gamma\in\{\ind{H},\ind{DH}\}$, where $\ind{H}$ and $\ind{DH}$ denote homodyne and double-homodyne detection, respectively, let $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}$ denote the one-mode Gaussian channel used in a channel representation of the corresponding target POVM.

A Gaussian channel $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}$ is characterized by real matrices $X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}$ and $Y_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}$. It transforms the first-moment vector $\boldsymbol{r}_G$ and covariance matrix $\Sigma_G$ of a Gaussian state as~\cite{Weed:rmp:2012}
\begin{align}
  &\boldsymbol{r}_G
  \mapsto
  X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}\boldsymbol{r}_G,
  \notag\\
  \label{eq:X,Y-action}
  &\Sigma_G
  \mapsto
  X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
  \Sigma_G
  \left[X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}\right]^{\ind{T}}
  +Y_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}.
\end{align}

For an arbitrary state $\hat{\rho}$ and POVM density
$\hat{\Pi}^{(\boldsymbol{u})}$, the outcome probability density
is~\cite{Nielsen:bk:2010}
\begin{align}
  p(\boldsymbol{u}|\hat{\rho})
  =
  \Tr\left[
    \hat{\rho}\,
    \hat{\Pi}^{(\boldsymbol{u})}
  \right].
\end{align}

We represent the target POVM by applying $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}$ to the input state before a reference Gaussian POVM is measured. Equivalently, the dual map $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}^{*}$ acts on the reference POVM:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:channel-motiv}
  p_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{u}|\hat{\rho})
  &=
  \Tr\left[
    \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho})\,
    \hat{\Pi}_{\ind{id}}^{(\boldsymbol{u})}
  \right]
  \notag\\
  &=
  \Tr\left[
    \hat{\rho}\,
    \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}^{*}
    \left(
      \hat{\Pi}_{\ind{id}}^{(\boldsymbol{u})}
    \right)
  \right].
\end{align}

For the channels considered below, we write
\begin{align}
  X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
  =
  \kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}\mathbbm{1},
  \qquad
  \kappa_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)}=1,
  \qquad
  \kappa_{\mathcal{E}}^{(\gamma)}=\cos\theta_{\gamma}.
\end{align}
Let $d_{\ind{H}}=1$ and $d_{\ind{DH}}=2$ denote the number of measured quadratures. We rescale the outcome of the reference POVM according to
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol{x}
  =
  \frac{\boldsymbol{u}}
  {\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}}.
\end{align}
For double-homodyne detection,
$\boldsymbol{u}=(z_1,z_2)^{\ind{T}}$, so that
\begin{align}
  z
  =
  \kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\ind{DH})}
  (x_1+i x_2).
\end{align}
The resulting POVM density with respect to
$\dd^{d_{\gamma}}\boldsymbol{x}$ is
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:rescaled-povm}
  \hat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}^{(\boldsymbol{x})}
  =
  \left[
    \kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
  \right]^{d_{\gamma}}
  \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}^{*}
  \left(
    \hat{\Pi}_{\ind{id}}^{
      \left(
        \kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
        \boldsymbol{x}
      \right)
    }
  \right).
\end{align}
The prefactor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rescaled-povm} is the Jacobian of the outcome rescaling and preserves the POVM normalization. A channel representation reproduces the target POVM if
\begin{align}
  \hat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}^{(\boldsymbol{x})}
  =
  \hat{\Pi}_{m}^{(\boldsymbol{x})}
\end{align}
for every outcome $\boldsymbol{x}$.

At the covariance-matrix level, this condition becomes
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:C-gen}
  \Sigma_m^{(\gamma)}
  =
  \left[
    \kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
  \right]^{-2}
  \left(
    \Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}
    +
    Y_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}
  \right).
\end{align}
Because the channels have zero displacement and
$X_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma)}$ is proportional to the identity, the outcome rescaling restores the input-dependent first moments. Equation~\eqref{eq:C-gen}, together with the Jacobian in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rescaled-povm}, therefore establishes equality with the normalized target Gaussian POVM.

\subsection{Additive-noise channel}
First, we consider the additive-noise channel $\Phi_{\gamma}$, characterized by the matrices~\cite{Serafini:bk:2023}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:X,Y-phi}
    X_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbbm{1}, \quad Y_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)}\succeq0, \quad \gamma\in\{\ind{H}, \ind{DH}\}.
\end{align}
The additive-noise channel is self-dual, $\cnj{\Phi}_\gamma=\Phi_\gamma$. It leaves the first moment unchanged and transforms the covariance matrix as
\begin{align}
  \Phi_\gamma:
  \Sigma\mapsto
  \Sigma+Y_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)}.
\end{align}

Equation~\eqref{eq:C-gen} requires
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:add-act}
  \Phi_{\gamma}:\quad \Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto\Sigma_m^{(\gamma)}=\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}+Y_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)},
\end{align}
and therefore
\begin{align}
  Y_{\Phi}^{(\gamma)}=\Sigma_{m}^{(\gamma)}-\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}\equiv \Sigma_{N}^{(\gamma)},
\end{align}
where $\Sigma_{N}^{(\gamma)}$ is the receiver excess-noise covariance matrix.

For homodyne detection, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sigma-H-id} and~\eqref{eq:Sigma-H-m} give
\begin{align}
    &
    \label{eq:Sigma-N-H}
    \Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{H})}=
    \Sigma_{m}^{(\ind{H})}-\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{H})}= R(\phi)
    \diag(\sigma_N, 0)
    \tcnj{R}(\phi)\succeq 0,
\end{align}
where $\sigma_N$ is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:sgm_N}.

For double-homodyne detection, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_m-DH} and~\eqref{eq:Sigma-id-DH} give
\begin{align}
  &
    \label{eq:Sigma-N-DH}
    \Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{DH})}(r)=
    \Sigma_{m}^{(\ind{DH})}-\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\ind{DH})}= R(\phi)
\begin{pmatrix}
      \sigma_N^{(1)}(r) &0\\0&\sigma_N^{(2)}(r)
    \end{pmatrix}
    \tcnj{R}(\phi)\succeq 0,\\
  \label{eq:sigma_N-sq-2}
  &
    \sigma_N^{(1)}(r)=\delta_1-e^{2r},
    \quad
    \sigma_N^{(2)}(r)=\delta_2-e^{-2r}.
\end{align}
The squeezing parameter $r$ determines the entries of $\Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{DH})}$. For every $r\in[r_2,r_1]$, the excess-noise covariance matrix is positive semidefinite, $\Sigma_{N}^{(\ind{DH})}(r)\succeq0$.

\subsection{Attenuator channel}
The attenuator channel $\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}$ is characterized by the matrices~\cite{Serafini:bk:2023}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:X,Y}
    X_{\mathcal{E}}^{(\gamma)}=\cos\theta_{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}, \quad Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{(\gamma)}=\sin^2\theta_{\gamma}\Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\gamma)}, \quad \gamma \in \{\ind{H}, \ind{DH}\},
\end{align}
where $\Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\gamma)}$ is the covariance matrix of the environmental input state.

Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:X,Y-action} and~\eqref{eq:C-gen}, we choose $\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}^{*}$ so that it maps the reference Gaussian POVM to the target POVM. In the rescaled outcome coordinates, its action on the covariance matrix is
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:psi_ast}
    \mathcal{E}_{\gamma}^{*}:\quad \Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto\Sigma_{m}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{\cos^2\theta_{\gamma}}\Sigma_{\ind{id}}^{(\gamma)}+\tan^2\theta_{\gamma}\Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\gamma)},
    \quad \gamma\in\{\ind{H},\ind{DH}\}.
\end{align}

For homodyne detection, we take the environmental input state to be the vacuum, with covariance matrix
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:sigma-anc-h}
    \Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\ind{H})}=\mathbbm{1}.
\end{align}
The attenuator transforms the measured quadrature according to
\begin{align}
  \hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{out}}
  =\cos\theta_{\ind{H}}\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{in}}
  +\sin\theta_{\ind{H}}\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{env}}.
\end{align}
Let $x_{\ind{raw}}$ denote the outcome associated with $\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{out}}$. The rescaling $x=x_{\ind{raw}}/\cos\theta_{\ind{H}}$ corresponds to the rescaled observable
\begin{align}
  \frac{\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{out}}}{\cos\theta_{\ind{H}}}
  =\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{in}}
  +\tan\theta_{\ind{H}}\hat{x}_{\phi,\ind{env}}.
\end{align}
Since the vacuum quadrature variance is one, the added variance is $\tan^2\theta_{\ind{H}}$. Exact equality with the target homodyne POVM therefore requires
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:tan2theta-h}
  \tan^2\theta_{\ind{H}}=\sigma_N. %\quad\Sigma_{\mathcal{E}_{\ind{H}}}\equiv R(\phi)\,\ind{diag}(\sigma_N,\sigma_N)\,{R}^{\ind{T}}(\phi).
\end{align}
Since $\sigma_x=1+\sigma_N$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:sgm_N}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tan2theta-h} gives
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:cos-theta-h}
    &\cos^2\theta_{\ind{H}}={\frac{1}{\sigma_x}},\quad
    \sin^2\theta_{\ind{H}}={\frac{\sigma_N}{\sigma_x}}.
\end{align}

For double-homodyne detection, first suppose that $r_2<r_1$, equivalently $\delta_1\delta_2>1$, and take $r\in(r_2,r_1)$. In the attenuator channel considered here, we take the environmental input state to be a centered pure Gaussian state with covariance matrix
%For double homodyne detection, first, we note that, since $\Sigma_m^{(\ind{DH})}$ is anisotropic, as generally quadrature variances $\delta_{1,2}$ are not equal, $\delta_1\neq\delta_2$, enviroment state must be anisotropic as well. Without loss of generality, we will consider the enviroment to be in pure state with $\det\Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\ind{DH})}=1$,
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:anc-dh-def}
    \Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\ind{DH})}(r)
    =R(\phi)\,\diag\left(a(r),a(r)^{-1}\right)\,\tcnj{R}(\phi),
    \qquad a(r)>0.
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:psi_ast}, \eqref{eq:Sigma-id-DH}, and~\eqref{eq:Sigma_m-DH} give
\begin{align}
  \Sigma_{m}^{(\ind{DH})}=
  R(\phi)\diag(\delta_1,\delta_2)\tcnj{R}(\phi)
  =R(\phi)\,\left\{
    \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)}\,\diag(e^{2r},e^{-2r})
    +
    \tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)\,\diag\left(a(r),a(r)^{-1}\right)
    \right\}\,\tcnj{R}(\phi),
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:system-dh}
    \begin{cases}
        \delta_1=\dfrac{e^{2r}}{\cos^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)}+\tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)a(r),\\[2mm]
        \delta_2=\dfrac{e^{-2r}}{\cos^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)}+\tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)a(r)^{-1}
    \end{cases}.
  \end{align}
Solving the system yields
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:cos-theta-dh}
    &\cos^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)={\frac{e^{2r}+a(r)}{\delta_1+a(r)}}=
    \frac{\delta_1 e^{-2r}+\delta_2e^{2r}-2}{\delta_1\delta_2-1}
    ,\\
    \label{eq:a_def}
    &a(r)=\frac{\delta_{1}-e^{2 r}}{\delta_{2} e^{2 r}-1}.%\frac{1}{2}\left[-b+ \sqrt{b^2-4c}\right],\quad
    %b=\frac{-\delta_1e^{-2r}+\delta_2e^{2r}}{\sigma_N^{(2)}},\quad c=\frac{-\sigma_N^{(1)}}{\sigma_N^{(2)}}
    %,\\
    %&\sigma_N^{(1)}=\delta_1-e^{2r}, \quad \sigma_N^{(2)}=\delta_2-e^{-2r}.
\end{align}
As $r\to r_2^+$ or $r\to r_1^-$, respectively, $a(r)$ or $a(r)^{-1}$ diverges while $\tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)\to0$. The products $\tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)a(r)$ and $\tan^2\theta_{\ind{DH}}(r)a(r)^{-1}$ approach finite limits. The environmental covariance matrix has no finite endpoint limit. The Gaussian channel has the finite limits
\begin{align}
  \lim_{r\to r_j}X_{\mathcal{E}}^{(\ind{DH})}(r)&=\mathbbm{1},
  &\lim_{r\to r_j}Y_{\mathcal{E}}^{(\ind{DH})}(r)&=\Sigma_N^{(\ind{DH})}(r_j).
\end{align}
We define $\mathcal E_{\ind{DH}}(r_j)$ by these one-sided channel limits. This continuous extension equals the corresponding additive-noise channel at each endpoint. If $r_1=r_2$, then $\delta_1\delta_2=1$, the target POVM is rank one, and both effective channels reduce to the identity channel.

\section{Gaussian-modulated coherent-state protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
In the Gaussian-modulated coherent-state (GMCS) protocol, Alice samples the complex amplitude $\alpha=\alpha_1+i\alpha_2$ from the Gaussian prior:
\begin{align}
  p_A(\alpha)
  =\frac{1}{2\pi V_{\ind{A}}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{|\alpha|^2}{2V_{\ind{A}}}\right].
\end{align}
She then prepares the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$.
The states pass through the phase-insensitive Gaussian communication channel $\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}$, specified by $X_{\mathcal{N}}=\sqrt{T_{\ind{ch}}}\mathbbm{1}$ and $Y_{\mathcal{N}}=(1-T_{\ind{ch}}+\xi_{\ind{ch}})\mathbbm{1}$, where $T_{\ind{ch}}$ is the channel transmittance and $\xi_{\ind{ch}}$ is the output-referred excess-noise variance~\cite{Laudenbach:aqt:2018}.
The receiver then uses homodyne or double-homodyne detection. The corresponding Alice--Bob mutual information is~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne}
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:I_H_res}
  &I_{\ind{AB}}^{(\ind{H})}=\frac{1}{2}\log_2\left[1+\frac{4T_{\ind{ch}}V_{\ind{A}}}{\sigma_x+\xi_{\ind{ch}}}\right],\\
  \label{eq:I_DH_res}
  &I_{\ind{AB}}^{(\ind{DH})}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\log_2\left[1+\frac{4T_{\ind{ch}}V_{\ind{A}}}
    {2 \sigma_i+\xi_{\ind{ch}}}\right],
\end{align}
where $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_i$ are given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sigma-x} and~\eqref{eq:sigm-i}, respectively. The superscripts $\ind{H}$ and $\ind{DH}$ label homodyne and double-homodyne detection throughout.

For the covariance-matrix calculations below, we apply the local quadrature transformation $R(\phi)$ on Alice's mode and $R(-\phi)$ on Bob's mode. This transformation preserves the diagonal local blocks and the correlation block proportional to $\sigma_z$, while diagonalizing the target and environmental covariance matrices. It does not change the symplectic eigenvalues or the information quantities. All covariance matrices below are expressed in this rotated basis.

The covariance matrix of a two-mode squeezed-vacuum (TMSV) state after Bob's mode passes through the communication channel is~\cite{Laudenbach:aqt:2018}
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:TMSV-to-AB}
  \mathcal{I}_A&\otimes \mathcal{N}_{A\to B}:
\quad
    \Sigma_{\ind{TMSV}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
        V\mathbbm{1}&\sqrt{V^2-1}\sigma_z\\
        \sqrt{V^2-1}\sigma_z&V\mathbbm{1}
    \end{pmatrix}
  \mapsto
    \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        V\mathbbm{1}&c\sigma_z\\
        c\sigma_z&V_{\ind{B}}\mathbbm{1}
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
where $\sigma_z=\diag(1,-1)$, $\mathcal{I}_A$ is the identity channel acting on subsystem $A$, and the parameters of $\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}$ are
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:V-c}
  &
    V=1+4V_{\ind{A}},
    \quad c=\sqrt{T_{\ind{ch}}(V^2-1)},
  \\
  &
  \label{eq:V_B}
    V_{\ind{B}}=T_{\ind{ch}}(V-1)+1+\xi_{\ind{ch}}.
\end{align}


We next apply either the additive-noise channel $\Phi_{\gamma}$~\eqref{eq:X,Y-phi} or the attenuator channel $\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}$~\eqref{eq:X,Y} to Bob's mode of the TMSV state~\eqref{eq:TMSV-to-AB}. For each channel, the corresponding reference POVM and outcome rescaling give the mutual information in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:I_H_res} and~\eqref{eq:I_DH_res} because they reproduce the same receiver outcome statistics.

It follows that
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:psi_action}
    &\mathcal{I}_A\otimes \mathcal{E}_{\gamma}:
    \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}\mapsto
     \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma})}
     =\begin{pmatrix}
        V\mathbbm{1}&c\cos\theta_{\gamma}\sigma_z\\
     c\cos\theta_{\gamma}\sigma_z&\cos^2\theta_{\gamma}
     \left(V_{\ind{B}}+\tan^2\theta_{\gamma}\Sigma_{\ind{env}}^{(\gamma)}\right)
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
\begin{align}
  &
  \label{eq:sigma_m_cov}
\mathcal{I}_A\otimes \Phi_{\gamma}:
  \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}\mapsto
  \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{(\Phi_{\gamma})}
  =\begin{pmatrix}
    V\mathbbm{1}&c\sigma_z\\
    c\sigma_z&V_{\ind{B}}\mathbbm{1}+\Sigma_N^{(\gamma)}
\end{pmatrix},\quad \gamma\in\{\ind{H},\ind{DH}\}.
\end{align}

In the untrusted-detector model adopted here, Eve holds a purification of the state
$\hat{\rho}_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}=
(\mathcal{I}_A\otimes \mathcal{C}_{\gamma})(\hat{\rho}_{\ind{AB}})$, and the receiver then applies the corresponding reference Gaussian POVM.
The difference between the joint and conditional entropies
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:chi}
  &
  \chi_{\ind{EB}} ^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}
    =
    S_{\ind{E}}-S_{\ind{E}|\ind{B}}=S_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}-S_{\ind{A}|\ind{B}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}, \quad \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\in\{\Phi_{\gamma}, \mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\}
\end{align}
gives the Holevo information.
The joint entropy
\begin{align}
  &
  \label{eq:S_AB-gamma}
  S_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}=g(\nu_1^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)})+g(\nu_2^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}),
  \\
  &\notag
    g(\nu)=\frac{\nu+1}{2}\log_2 \frac{\nu+1}{2}-
    \frac{\nu-1}{2}\log_2\frac{\nu-1}{2}
\end{align}
is determined by the symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrices in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:psi_action} and~\eqref{eq:sigma_m_cov}:
\begin{align}
  &
    \label{eq:nu-12-gamma}
\nu_{1,2}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}
    =\sqrt{
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}/2\pm \sqrt{\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}^2/4-D_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}}
    },
    \quad
  D_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}=\det \Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)},
    \\
  &
  \label{eq:D-gamma}
  \Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}}=\det([\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}]_{11})+\det([\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}]_{22}) +
  2\det([\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}]_{12}),
\end{align}
where $[\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}]_{ii}$ ($[\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}]_{ij}$) are diagonal (off-diagonal) blocks of $\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}$.

The conditional entropies $S_{\ind{E|B}}=S_{\ind{A|B}}^{(\gamma)}$ are calculated from the corresponding conditional covariance matrices,
\begin{align}
  &
    \label{eq:S_A1B-gamma}
    S_{\ind{A|B}}^{(\gamma)}=g(\nu_3^{(\gamma)}),\quad
    \nu_3^{(\gamma)}=\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{\ind{A|B}}^{(\gamma)}},\\
&  \label{eq:serf-a|b}
  \Sigma_{\ind{A|B}}^{(\gamma)}=
  V\mathbbm{1}-c^2\sigma_z(V_{\ind{B}}\mathbbm{1}+\Sigma^{(\gamma)}_m)^{-1}\sigma_z.
\end{align}
Direct computations show that these conditional matrices, and therefore their symplectic eigenvalues, are independent of which effective receiver channel is used. The symplectic eigenvalues are
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:nu3-H}
    &\nu_3^{(\ind{H})}=\sqrt{V\left(V-\frac{c^2}{V_{\ind{B}}+\sigma_N}\right)},\\
    \label{eq:nu3-DH}
  &
    \nu_3^{(\ind{DH})}=V
    \sqrt{\frac{\left(V_{\ind{B}}  + \delta_1 - \frac{c^{2}}V\right) \left( V_{\ind{B}}  + \delta_2
    - \frac{c^{2}}V\right)}{
    \left( V_{\ind{B}} + \delta_1\right) \left(V_{\ind{B}} + \delta_2\right)}},
\end{align}

The asymptotic secret fraction is given by the Devetak--Winter formula~\cite{Devetak:prsa:2005}
\begin{align}
\label{eq:r}
K^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)}=\beta I_{AB}^{(\gamma)}-\chi_{EB}^{\left(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\right)},
\quad \gamma\in\{\ind{H},\ind{DH}\}, \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\in\{\Phi_{\gamma}, \mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\}.
\end{align}
Here $\beta\in(0,1]$ is the reverse-reconciliation efficiency.

The additive-noise symplectic eigenvalues obtained from Eq.~\eqref{eq:nu-12-gamma} do not simplify further. The following sections give analytic expressions only for the attenuator channel.

\subsection{Homodyne detection}
For the attenuator channel, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:psi_action} and~\eqref{eq:cos-theta-h} give
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:psi-h-action}
    &\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{(\mathcal{E}_{\ind{H}})}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        V\mathbbm{1}&\frac{c}{\sqrt{\sigma_x}}\sigma_z\\
        \frac{c}{\sqrt{\sigma_x}}\sigma_z&\frac{1}{{\sigma_x}}\left(V_{\ind{B}}+\sigma_N\right)\mathbbm{1}
    \end{pmatrix}\equiv
    \begin{pmatrix}
        V\mathbbm{1}&\tilde{c}\sigma_z\\
        \tilde{c}\sigma_z&\tilde{V}_{\ind{B}}\mathbbm{1}
    \end{pmatrix},\\
    &\tilde{V}_{\ind{B}}\equiv
    {T}(V-1)+1+{\xi}
    ,\\
    &\tilde{c}\equiv\sqrt{{T}(V^2-1)},
\end{align}
where $\tilde{V}_{\ind{B}}$ is Bob's scaled variance and $\tilde{c}$ is the scaled Alice--Bob correlation amplitude. The effective transmittance $T$ and excess-noise variance $\xi$ are
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:tot_hom}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:T_tot_hom}
    &T=T_{\ind{ch}}\cdot \frac{1}{\sigma_x},\\
    \label{eq:xi_tot_hom}
    &\xi\equiv\xi_{\ind{ch}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma_x}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

For homodyne detection, receiver asymmetry therefore scales both the effective transmittance~\eqref{eq:T_tot_hom} and the output-referred excess noise~\eqref{eq:xi_tot_hom} by $\sigma_x^{-1}$.

The symplectic eigenvalues of $\Sigma_{\ind{AB}}^{(\mathcal{E}_{\ind{H}})}$ are~\cite{Weed:rmp:2012}
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:lambda12-h}
    \nu_{1,2}^{(\mathcal{E}_{\ind{H}})}=\frac{\omega_{\ind H}\pm[\tilde{V}_{\ind{B}}-V]}2,\quad
    \omega_{\ind H}=\sqrt{\left(V+\tilde{V}_{\ind{B}}\right)^2-4{\tilde{c}^2}}.
\end{align}
These eigenvalues are then substituted into Eq.~\eqref{eq:S_AB-gamma} to obtain the joint entropy $S_{\ind{AB}}^{(\mathcal{E}_{\ind{H}})}$.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we have constructed and compared additive-noise and
attenuator channel representations of the target homodyne and
double-homodyne POVMs. Our approach requires the dual map of the selected Gaussian channel, together with a
deterministic invertible rescaling of the measurement outcomes, to
reproduce the target POVM. At the level of covariance matrices, this condition is expressed by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:C-gen}. It constrains the channel parameters, which are then determined by the receiver parameters and, for double-homodyne detection, by the squeezing parameter $r$.


After the deterministic invertible rescaling of the measurement
outcomes, both channels reproduce the same conditional
statistics of Bob's outcomes. They therefore give the same Alice-Bob mutual information.
For fixed source and channel parameters, the conditional
covariance matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:serf-a|b}, and hence the conditional
entropy $S_{A|B}^{(\gamma)}$, are the same for both channels because the
detector imperfections enter the conditional state through the same
target POVM. The difference between the security estimates originates from the joint state of Alice and Bob. The additive-noise and attenuator channels generally produce different joint covariance matrices and, consequently, different joint entropies. In the untrusted-detector model considered here, Eve holds a purification of the Alice--Bob state after the effective receiver channel. The corresponding Holevo information~\eqref{eq:chi} can therefore depend on the selected channel while Bob's observed statistics remain unchanged.


For homodyne detection, we take the environmental mode of the attenuator channel to be in the
vacuum state, so that the detector imperfections are represented by a
pure-loss channel. This
channel rescales both the effective transmission and the
output-referred excess noise by the same factor, as shown in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tot_hom}.

The numerical results in Fig.~\ref{fig:h-comparison-CM} show that the additive-noise and
attenuator channels give different security estimates. At high channel
transmittance and for the parameters considered, the attenuator
channel gives a larger Holevo information and a lower asymptotic
secret fraction. Its secret fraction remains positive over a greater channel length. Thus, the channel giving the lower secret fraction changes with the communication distance.

A distinguishing feature of double-homodyne detection is the
nonuniqueness of the channel representation of its target POVM. The covariance
matrix of the target double-homodyne POVM is fixed
by the receiver parameters and does not depend on the squeezing
parameter $r$. The effects of the reference Gaussian POVM are proportional to projectors onto displaced squeezed states and depend on $r$. The target POVM is obtained by applying the dual map of the selected Gaussian channel to the reference Gaussian POVM, together with the outcome rescaling. The requirement that the excess-noise covariance matrix be positive
semidefinite restricts $r$ to the interval given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:r1-r2}.

For the additive-noise channel, variation of $r$ redistributes the
measurement noise between the two conjugate quadratures according to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_N-sq-2}. For the attenuator channel, the same variation changes
both the attenuation coefficient and
the covariance matrix of the environmental state, as described by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cos-theta-dh} and~\eqref{eq:a_def}. In the attenuator channel considered here, the generally anisotropic
measurement noise is reproduced by an environmental mode in an anisotropic Gaussian
state. We choose this environmental state to be pure.

When the admissible interval for $r$ has nonzero width, one additive-noise quadrature variance vanishes at each boundary. The environmental state in the attenuator channel then approaches an infinitely squeezed limit, with one quadrature variance tending to zero and the conjugate variance diverging. At each boundary, the attenuator channel converges to the corresponding additive-noise channel. If the interval collapses to a single value, the target POVM is rank one and both effective channels reduce to the identity channel.

Figure~\ref{fig:Entropy} shows that the Holevo information depends on
$r$ in qualitatively different ways for the two channels.
For the parameter sets considered, this dependence is concave for
the additive-noise channel and convex for the attenuator
channel. When the measurement noise is symmetric between the two
quadratures, both curves are symmetric about $r=0$. In this case, the coherent-state POVM at $r=0$ maximizes the Holevo
information for the additive-noise channel and minimizes it for the
attenuator channel.
For the asymmetric case considered, this symmetry is lost and the
extrema are shifted away from $r=0$.

The lower and upper secret-fraction curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:K-vs-L-q-add} describe the ranges obtained by varying $r$ over its admissible interval for the two channels. For the near-ideal quadrature variances used in Fig.~\ref{fig:K-vs-L-q-add}, the additive-noise range is narrow. The lower bound of the continuously extended attenuator curve occurs at an endpoint, where the limiting channel equals the corresponding additive-noise channel. These facts make the lower bounds nearly coincide. For both imbalance ratios shown, the attenuator range is wider than the additive-noise range. Increasing the imbalance from $q=1$ to $q=1.25$ makes the additive-noise range more pronounced.

The comparison shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dh-comparison-CM} was performed at
the midpoint of the admissible interval of $r$. In the symmetric case, this value coincides with the
maximum of the additive-noise Holevo information and the minimum of
the attenuator Holevo information. The pronounced separation between
the secret fractions in Fig.~\ref{fig:dh-comparison-CM} therefore reflects both the choice of effective channel and the selected squeezing parameter.

These results clarify the role of the effective receiver channel when receiver noise is treated as untrusted. The additive-noise and attenuator channels reproduce the same receiver statistics after outcome rescaling. They generally yield different joint states of Alice and Bob. In the untrusted-detector model, Eve holds a purification of the state produced by the selected channel. Selecting that channel therefore fixes an assumption in the security analysis.
The effective receiver channel should follow from the physical structure of the receiver. If several channel representations remain compatible with the receiver statistics, the analysis should report the dependence of the security estimate on that choice. The present comparison covers the additive-noise channels defined above and attenuator channels with pure Gaussian environmental input states for $r\in(r_2,r_1)$, together with their endpoint limits.

Appendix~\ref{sec:lossy-bs} extends the receiver model to include independent pure losses at the output ports of a lossless beam splitter. These losses can be incorporated into the effective photodetector efficiencies. For a general lossy asymmetric beam splitter, the positive factor in the polar decomposition need not be diagonal in the output basis and therefore cannot be absorbed into independent detector's efficiency replacements.

The security analysis treats the GMCS CV-QKD protocol in the asymptotic regime under collective Gaussian attacks and the untrusted-detector model. Under these assumptions, identical receiver statistics can lead to different estimates of Eve's information for different effective receiver channels.

\section{Lossy beam splitter}
\label{sec:lossy-bs}
In this appendix, %we discuss how the formulas describing homodyne and double-homodyne detection cases relate to the case if beam splitters are lossy.
we show that independent pure-loss channels at the output ports of a lossless beam splitter are equivalent to a single lossy beam splitter.

First, consider the case of homodyne detection.
The lossless unbalanced beam splitter mixes the input signal mode $\hat{a}$ and the local oscillator mode $\hat{a}_L$. This transformation is described by the unitary rotation matrix $\vc{U}_{\ind{BS}}$,
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a_{1}\\
        \hat a_{2}
    \end{pmatrix}
    &=
    \vc{U}_{\ind{BS}}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a\\
        \hat a_{L}
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        C & S \\
        -S & C
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a\\
        \hat a_{L}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        C\hat{a}+S\hat{a}_L\\
        -S\hat{a}+C\hat{a}_L
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
where $C$ and $S$ are the amplitude transmission and reflection coefficients of the beam splitter BS, respectively.

We model the subsequent losses by passing each intermediate output mode $\hat{a}_{1,2}$ through a virtual beam splitter of transmittance $\tau_{1,2}$. This mixes each signal with an independent environmental vacuum mode $\hat{v}_{1,2}$, where $[\hat{v}_i, \hat{v}_j^\dag] = \delta_{ij}$,
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat b_{1}\\
        \hat c_{1}
    \end{pmatrix}&=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_1}&-\sqrt{1-\tau_1}\\\sqrt{1-\tau_1}&\sqrt{\tau_1}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a_1\\
        \hat v_1
    \end{pmatrix},\\
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat b_{2}\\
        \hat c_{2}
    \end{pmatrix}&=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_2}&-\sqrt{1-\tau_2}\\\sqrt{1-\tau_2}&\sqrt{\tau_2}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a_2\\
        \hat v_2
    \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Tracing out the lost environmental modes $\hat{c}_{1,2}$, the remaining physical output modes $\hat{b}_{1,2}$ expand to
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:b1b2-out}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat b_{1}\\
        \hat b_{2}
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_1}C & \sqrt{\tau_1}S\\
        -\sqrt{\tau_2}S & \sqrt{\tau_2}C
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat{a}\\
        \hat{a}_L
    \end{pmatrix}
    +
    \begin{pmatrix}
        -\sqrt{1-\tau_1}\hat{v}_1\\
        -\sqrt{1-\tau_2}\hat{v}_2
    \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:b1b2-out}, the effective scattering matrix is
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:T_eff_extract}
    \vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_1}C & \sqrt{\tau_1}S\\
        -\sqrt{\tau_2}S & \sqrt{\tau_2}C
    \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
The associated noise operators are $\hat F_i=-\sqrt{1-\tau_i}\,\hat v_i$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$.

Preservation of the canonical commutation relations $[\hat{b}_i, \hat{b}_j^\dagger] = \delta_{ij}$ for the output fields requires
\begin{align}
  [\hat F_i, \hat F_j^\dagger] = \delta_{ij} - \sum_{k} T_{ik} T_{jk}^*,
\end{align}
where $T_{ik}$ are the elements of the scattering matrix $\vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}$~\eqref{eq:T_eff_extract}.
Evaluating this constraint explicitly using the elements of $\vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}$ yields:
\begin{align}
    &[\hat F_1, \hat F_1^\dagger] = %1 - |T_{11}|^2 - |T_{12}|^2 = 1-\tau_1 C^2-\tau_1 S^2 = 
    1-\tau_1,\\
    &[\hat F_2, \hat F_2^\dagger] = %1 - |T_{21}|^2 - |T_{22}|^2 = 1-\tau_2 S^2-\tau_2 C^2 = 
    1-\tau_2,\\
    &[\hat F_1, \hat F_2^\dagger] = %0 - (T_{11}T_{21}^* + T_{12}T_{22}^*) = -\left[(\sqrt{\tau_1}C)(\sqrt{\tau_2}S) + (-\sqrt{\tau_1}S)(\sqrt{\tau_2}C)\right] = 
    0,
\end{align}
which matches the commutators calculated from Eq.~\eqref{eq:b1b2-out}.


We now relate this setup to the standard input-output description of lossy beam splitters~\cite{Barnett1998,Knll1999}. This description uses an interaction matrix $\vc{A}$ acting on ancillary modes and requires $\vc{1}-\vc{A}\hcnj{\vc{A}}\succeq 0$. The scattering matrix $\vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_eff_extract} factors into a diagonal attenuation matrix and the lossless unitary:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:R_eff}
    \vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_1} & 0\\
        0 & \sqrt{\tau_2}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        C & S\\
        -S & C
    \end{pmatrix}
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{\tau_1} & 0\\
        0 & \sqrt{\tau_2}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \vc{U}_{\ind{BS}}.
\end{align}
For $\vc{A}\hcnj{\vc{A}}=\diag(1-\tau_1,1-\tau_2)$,
\begin{align}
    \sqrt{\vc{1}-\vc{A}\hcnj{\vc{A}}} = \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{\tau_1} & 0 \\\\ 0 & \sqrt{\tau_2} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:R_eff} then gives the polar decomposition~\cite{Knll1999}
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:lossy-T}
  \vc{T}_{\ind{BS}}=\sqrt{\vc{1}-\vc{A}\hcnj{\vc{A}}}\,\vc{U}_{\ind{BS}}.
\end{align}
Because $\vc{A}\hcnj{\vc{A}}$ is diagonal, the two output losses can be absorbed into the detector efficiencies as $\eta_{1,2}\to\tau_{1,2}\eta_{1,2}$. The same rescaling applies to double-homodyne detection.

We finally consider a general lossy asymmetric beam splitter with scattering matrix~\cite{Uppu_2016}
\begin{align}
  \vc{S}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    t & \rho\\
    r_{\ind{BS}} & \tau
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \vc{S}\hcnj{\vc{S}}\leq\vc{1},
\end{align}
where $t$ and $\tau$ are complex transmission amplitudes, while $\rho$ and $r_{\ind{BS}}$ are complex reflection amplitudes.\ %, and $\phi_{1,2}$ are the phase shifts.
A general $\vc S$ also admits a polar decomposition. Its positive factor need not be diagonal in the output basis, so it cannot be absorbed into two detector-efficiency replacements. The same obstruction appears in the independent interference coefficients in $\sigma_G$ and $\mu_G$ below:
\begin{align}
  %\label{eq:BS}
  &
    \ket{\alpha,\alpha_L}\mapsto\ket{\alpha_{\ind{out},1},\alpha_{\ind{out},2}},
  \\
  &
    %\label{eq:amplitudes}
    \alpha_{\ind{out},1}=t\alpha+\rho \alpha_L,\quad
    \alpha_{\ind{out},2}=r_{\ind{BS}}\alpha+\tau\alpha_L,
\end{align}

We use the normalized Gaussian probability density
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:G-notation}
  G(x;\sigma)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma}\right).
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
  &
  %\label{eq:Gaussian}
    \prob_G(\mu)=G(\mu-\mu_G;\sigma_G),
    \\
    \label{eq:sigma_G-general-lossy-bs}
    \sigma_G
    &=
    \eta_1|\alpha_{\ind{out},1}|^2+\eta_2|\alpha_{\ind{out},2}|^2
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \left(
        \eta_1|\rho|^2+\eta_2|\tau|^2
    \right)|\alpha_L|^2
    +
    \left(
        \eta_1|t|^2+\eta_2|r_{\ind{BS}}|^2
    \right)|\alpha|^2
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[
        \left(
            \eta_1t\rho^*
            +
            \eta_2r_{\ind{BS}}\tau^*
        \right)
        \alpha\alpha_L^*
    \right]
    \nonumber\\
    &\approx
    \left(
        \eta_1|\rho|^2+\eta_2|\tau|^2
    \right)|\alpha_L|^2
    +
    2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[
        \left(
            \eta_1t\rho^*
            +
            \eta_2r_{\ind{BS}}\tau^*
        \right)
        \alpha\alpha_L^*
    \right],
    \\
    \label{eq:mu_G-general-lossy-bs}
    \mu_G
    &=
    \eta_1|\alpha_{\ind{out},1}|^2-\eta_2|\alpha_{\ind{out},2}|^2
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \left(
        \eta_1|\rho|^2-\eta_2|\tau|^2
    \right)|\alpha_L|^2
    +
    \left(
        \eta_1|t|^2-\eta_2|r_{\ind{BS}}|^2
    \right)|\alpha|^2
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[
        \left(
            \eta_1t\rho^*
            -
            \eta_2r_{\ind{BS}}\tau^*
        \right)
        \alpha\alpha_L^*
    \right]
    \nonumber\\
    &\approx
    \left(
        \eta_1|\rho|^2-\eta_2|\tau|^2
    \right)|\alpha_L|^2
    +
    2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[
        \left(
            \eta_1t\rho^*
            -
            \eta_2r_{\ind{BS}}\tau^*
        \right)
        \alpha\alpha_L^*
    \right].
\end{align}

\section{Fluctuating local oscillator}\label{sec:fluc-LO}
In this appendix, we extend the homodyne model to include classical amplitude fluctuations of a phase-locked local oscillator (LO).% In the main text, the LO amplitude $\vert\alpha_L\vert$ is treated as a fixed constant. Here, we model it as a fluctuating quantity and evaluate its impact on the Gaussian-approximated POVM.

Let $L=|\alpha_L|$ denote the mean LO amplitude. For fixed $L$, the photon-count-difference distribution in the strong-LO Gaussian approximation is~\cite{naumchik2025asymmetryeffectshomodyneheterodyne}
\begin{align}
  &
  \label{eq:Gaussian}
    \prob_G(\mu\mid L)=G(\mu-\mu_G(L);\sigma_G(L)),
    \\
  &
    \label{eq:sigma_G}
    \sigma_G(L)\approx
    (\eta_1S^2+\eta_2C^2)L^2,
  \\
  &
    \label{eq:mu_G}
    \mu_G(L)\approx
    (\eta_1S^2-\eta_2C^2)L^2
    +CS (\eta_1+\eta_2)L\avr{\hat{x}_\phi}.
\end{align}
Here $G$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:G-notation}.

We write the instantaneous LO amplitude as $L+\Delta_L$, where $\Delta_L$ is a zero-mean Gaussian random variable:
\begin{align}
  p_L(\Delta_L)=G(\Delta_L;\sigma_L),
  \qquad \sqrt{\sigma_L}\ll L.
\end{align}
Here $\sigma_L$ is the variance of the LO amplitude fluctuations. Under the small-fluctuation condition, extending the integral over $\Delta_L$ to the full real line introduces a negligible tail with $L+\Delta_L<0$.

The unconditional photon-count-difference distribution is the Gaussian mixture:% We assume the phase $\phi = \arg\alpha_L$ is locked, so that the amplitude fluctiation $\delta\alpha_L$ is real-valued random variable.
\begin{align}

\end{align}

\begin{align}
